The one rule

Every element above the fold earns its slot with a metric it moves

Today's home has eleven blocks in the first viewport and two of them are food. The proposed home has seven, and four of them are stores you can tap. The store page moves the first dish from about 80% of the viewport to under 50%. The menu goes from three navigation systems to one. None of it removes an ad product; it makes each impression worth more.

Why it works

Twelve reasons, each tied to a numbered change

The effects behind the redesign in plain terms. Click a tag to jump to that change. H = Home, S = Store page, M = Menu.

Fewer options, faster decisions

The time and effort it takes to decide grows with every extra option on screen. Cutting a rail from 14 to 6 speeds up the choice.

People hold a handful of things in mind

A card with seven signals asks the reader to hold seven things at once. Two get read; seven get skipped.

Anything shaped like an ad gets skipped

Most of all at the top of a page. A smaller, labeled card inside the feed gets looked at; a full-height hero gets scrolled past.

One highlight stands out, ten cancel out

A promo tag draws the eye only when it is rare. When every item, banner and bar is red, none of them is.

First position gets remembered

What sits first on the screen is what people notice and recall. That slot should hold the user's intent, not the ad.

Progress pulls people forward

Once one BOGO item is in the cart, "one more" makes the second add far likelier than asking for two up front.

Unfinished things stay in mind

"1 of 2" is a hook the user wants to close. "Add 2 eligible items" is a chore.

Show the next step, not every step

Filters, store info and Ask are there when asked for and out of the way otherwise.

Close, large targets are faster to hit

The bottom search pill sits under the thumb. That is the right call and it stays.

People expect your app to work like the ones they know

"Order again" reads instantly because "Buy again" already taught it.

Experiences are judged by their peak and their end

Ask for a photo when the food arrives and the user is happiest, not while they are still choosing.

Complexity moves, it doesn't vanish

Put it where intent is (a filter icon, a "more" menu) instead of on every screen.

The formal names, if anyone asks, in the same order: Hick's law, Miller's law, banner blindness, the Von Restorff effect, the serial-position effect, the goal-gradient effect, the Zeigarnik effect, progressive disclosure, Fitts's law, Jakob's law, the peak-end rule, Tesler's law. Reference: growth.design/psychology.

Experiment plan

Three staged A/B tests, each with an ad-revenue guardrail

Consumer and Ads are measured on the same scorecard instead of fighting over the fold.

Test 1 · Home

Intent-first home

Hypothesis Returning users convert more when Order again precedes Spotlight, and Spotlight's CTR rises when the unit is smaller, contextual and dismissible.

Primary session → order conversion

Secondary time to first store tap, reorder rate, search starts

Guardrails Spotlight revenue per session (non-inferior, 2% bound), new-vertical visits, DashPass sign-ups

Test 2 · Store page

Compressed header, one offer line

Hypothesis Rating and ETA in the header plus a single offer line raise first-add rate without lowering offer awareness.

Primary store view → first add

Secondary menu scroll depth, pickup share, offer-sheet opens

Guardrails promo cost per order, group-order starts, share rate

Test 3 · Menu + cart bar

Goal-gradient cart bar

Hypothesis A cart bar that shows "1 of 2" after the first add completes more BOGOs than a pre-emptive sticky banner.

Primary BOGO completion among sessions with one eligible add

Secondary AOV, items per order, DashPass threshold attainment

Guardrails cart abandonment, photo submissions per 1k orders (3b: post-delivery ask)

Segments and rollout

New vs returning (the home layout differs by design). DashPass vs not. Mealtime vs off-peak. iOS vs Android. Ramp 5% → 25% → 50%, two weeks each; keep a 5% holdout for eight weeks to read retention, not just conversion.

Why Ads should want this

Fewer, labeled placements with a dismiss signal improve targeting and CTR. "Deals near you" adds sponsored-listing inventory to the first two screens. Spotlight stays a premium unit and gains a viewability rule (100% in view, one second) that advertisers can be billed on with confidence.

Kill criteria Any test that moves ad revenue per session below the 2% bound for two consecutive weeks reverts, and the fold rule is renegotiated with Ads before a second attempt.
Evidence

The constraint, what people say, and a precedent inside DoorDash

1 · The constraint

Why the hero can't be deleted

Spotlight is DoorDash Ads' premium homepage unit: "a large, immersive creative canvas" for static display and short video. DoorDash reports "2.5x higher click-through rates" than banners, "+20% new-to-merchant sales" for restaurants, "+36% new-to-brand sales" for CPG, and a "60%+ video completion rate".

DoorDash and Wolt Ads passed a $1B annualized run rate in 2024. In June 2026 the platform unified DoorDash, Wolt and Deliveroo ads for 400,000+ advertisers.

Sources: DoorDash Ads, "Introducing Spotlight"; "DoorDash Ads Becomes a Global Commerce Media Platform", 4 Jun 2026.

Implication The redesign constrains the unit and makes each impression worth more. It does not remove it.

2 · What people say

Reddit and Blind

r/doordash · "Pickup and Map option completely removed in new UI?!" A redesign hid pickup and the map behind search → Browse. Lesson: hide noise, never tools.

r/doordash · "App is hiding stores" Discoverability complaints when the feed prioritizes modules over stores.

r/doordash_drivers · "So frequently the UI changes feel overlooked…" "The design feels cluttered and harder to navigate." Dasher app, same organization.

r/ios · "iOS just served me an ad on my home screen" (160+ comments) A DoorDash App Clip ad. Ad aggression is already a brand-trust cost.

Blind · "DoorDash app product feedback" (Mar 2024) "Poorly organized menu… can deter me from a place." Reviews "could be more front and center."

3 · Internal precedent

The Dasher home did this already

"DoorDash Visions: redesigning the Dasher app's homescreen" (Akhil Dakinedi, Medium). The Dasher home "quickly got cluttered" with entry points, features and CTAs because of "a lack of ownership and governance"; banners and tooltips were moved into an Inbox to clear it.

Implication The consumer home needs the same governance rule: a slot above the fold is earned by a metric, reviewed quarterly, and the notifications inbox is where campaign messages go when they lose it.
