Working Backwards as a Claude Skill

Press release to pull request. Seven human gates.

See it live GitHub

The problem

AI-assisted development breaks the old lifecycle: a model's output can be wrong in ways a test never sees. So a feature needs a human review at every point where a model's work enters the product, and it needs a definition sharp enough that every later gate can check against it.

Seven gates, and the skill at the first one

  • Gate 1, feature review, and gate 2, acceptance criteria were mine. I ran Working Backwards sessions to turn a fuzzy problem into a press release, FAQs and requirements that each cite the customer narrative they came from. On one network-onboarding feature that produced 104 acceptance criteria across about 20 areas.
  • Gates 3 to 7: design, code, test, dependencies, pull requests, owned by engineering, each ending in a named reviewer signing off on what the AI produced.
  • The rollout. The framework came from engineering leadership; I led my team's adoption, proved it on a feature that took network onboarding from 8 weeks to 1 day, and ran the enablement that took it to 50+ product managers.

The skill, rebuilt in the open

On my own time I rebuilt the Working Backwards part as an open-source Claude Skill: nine gated stages from press release to engineer-ready requirements and a tracker import, with a separate judge reviewing every stage. Its evals are published, misses included: the judge agrees with human labels on 14 of 14 cases, and the author prompts pass 25 of 26 contract properties.

What I would flag

  • Every gate ends in a person. The speed is a by-product; the point is that nothing AI-generated reaches production without a named reviewer.
  • The open-source skill is my own re-creation of the method, with fictional worked examples. Nothing employer-internal is in it.
